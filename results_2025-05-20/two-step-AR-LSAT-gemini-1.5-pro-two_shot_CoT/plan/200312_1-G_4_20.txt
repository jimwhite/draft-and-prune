2. Variables:

3. Constraints:

    - Constraint 2 (Kramer and Lopez review fewer plays than Megregian):

        - `num_reviews(2) < num_reviews(3)`
    - Constraint 3 (Neither Lopez nor Megregian reviews any play Jiang reviews):
        - `ForAll([p], Implies(reviews[0][p], Not(reviews[2][p])))` for p in range(3)

    - Constraint 4 (Kramer and O'Neill both review Tamerlane):

        - `reviews[4][1]`
    - Constraint 5 (Exactly two students review the same plays):
        - `PbEq([(And(ForAll([p], reviews[i][p] == reviews[j][p]), i!=j), 1) for i in range(5) for j in range(5)], 2)`

4. Answering the Question:
    - Create a base Z3 solver with constraints 1-5.
    - Define the number of reviews for each student: `num_reviews = lambda s: Sum([If(reviews[s][p], 1, 0) for p in range(3)])`
    - Formulate the constraint for each answer choice:
        - A: `num_reviews(0) > num_reviews(2)` (Jiang reviews more than Lopez)
        - B: `num_reviews(3) > num_reviews(0)` (Megregian reviews more than Jiang)
        - C: `num_reviews(3) > num_reviews(4)` (Megregian reviews more than O'Neill)
        - D: `num_reviews(4) > num_reviews(0)` (O'Neill reviews more than Jiang)
        - E: `num_reviews(4) > num_reviews(1)` (O'Neill reviews more than Kramer)
    - For each answer choice constraint `C`:
        - Push the solver state.
        - Add the negation of the answer choice constraint: `Not(C)`.
        - Check `solver.check()`.
        - If `solver.check() == unsat`, then the original constraint `C` must be true. This is the correct answer.
        - Pop the solver state.
    - The answer choice whose negation leads to an unsatisfiable state is the one that must be true.
