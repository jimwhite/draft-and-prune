2.  **Define Variables and Domain:**  
    * Define the variables as the seven fruits: `"mangoes"`, `"watermelons"`, `"peaches"`, `"kiwis"`, `"oranges"`, `"cantaloupes"`, and `"plums"`.  
    * Define the domain as integers from 1 to 7, where **1 represents the cheapest** and **7 represents the most expensive**.  
    * Add all variables with this domain to the `Problem` instance.

3.  **Formulate and Add Constraints:**  
    * Add an `AllDifferentConstraint` to enforce uniqueness of prices (no two fruits share the same price rank).  
    * Translate each statement into a constraint:  
        * "The watermelons are the cheapest" → `watermelons == 1`.  
        * "The peaches are more expensive than the mangoes" → `mangoes < peaches`.  
        * "The cantaloupes are the second-most expensive" → `cantaloupes == 6` (since most expensive is 7, second-most is 6).  
        * "The oranges are more expensive than the cantaloupes" → `cantaloupes < oranges`.  
        * "The peaches are less expensive than the plums" → `peaches < plums`.  
        * "The kiwis are the third-cheapest" → `kiwis == 3` (since cheapest=1, second=2, third=3).  

4.  **Solve the Problem:**  
    * Call `getSolutions()` to find all valid assignments.  
    * Extract the unique solution (as the constraints fully determine a single ordering).

5.  **Determine the Correct Answer:**

    * Build a mapping from choice letters (`"A"` through `"G"`) to fruit names as given in the choices.
