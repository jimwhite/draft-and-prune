2. **Define Variables and Domain:**

   * Define the domain as integers 1 through 7, where **1 = cheapest** and **7 = most expensive**.  
   * Add all seven variables with domain {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7} to the `Problem` instance.

3. **Formulate and Add Constraints:**  
   * Add an `AllDifferentConstraint` to enforce unique price ranks.  
   * Translate each statement into a constraint using lambda functions:  
     - "The loquats are less expensive than the oranges" → `loquats < oranges`  
     - "The oranges are the third-most expensive" → `oranges == 5` (since most expensive = 7, so third-most expensive = 7 − 2 = 5)  
     - "The apples are the cheapest" → `apples == 1`  
     - "The kiwis are more expensive than the pears" → `kiwis > pears`  
     - "The mangoes are more expensive than the watermelons" → `mangoes > watermelons`  
     - "The loquats are more expensive than the kiwis" → `loquats > kiwis`  

4. **Solve for the Unique Arrangement:**  
   * Call `getSolutions()` to find all valid assignments.  
   * Extract the solution (the problem is guaranteed to have a unique arrangement).
