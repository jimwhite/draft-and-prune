2. **Define Variables and Domain:**  
   * Define the five fruits as variables: `"mangoes"`, `"kiwis"`, `"plums"`, `"pears"`, and `"watermelons"`.  
   * Assign a domain of integers from 1 to 5, where **1 = cheapest**, **2 = second-cheapest**, **3 = third-most expensive**, **4 = second-most expensive**, and **5 = most expensive** (i.e., price rank increases left to right in terms of cost).  
   * Add these variables and their domain (1–5) to the `Problem` instance.

3. **Translate Statements into Constraints:**  
   * Add an `AllDifferentConstraint` to enforce unique price ranks for each fruit.  
   * Convert each statement into a constraint:  
        - *"The kiwis are less expensive than the plums"* → `kiwis < plums` (lower rank number = cheaper).  
        - *"The pears are the third-most expensive"* → `pears == 3`.  
        - *"The kiwis are the second-cheapest"* → `kiwis == 2`.  
        - *"The watermelons are the most expensive"* → `watermelons == 5`.

4. **Solve the Constraint Problem:**  
   * Use `getSolutions()` to find all valid assignments.  
   * Extract the unique solution (since constraints are sufficient for a single arrangement).

5. **Evaluate the Question and Choices:**  
   * The question asks which fruit is the third-most expensive → i.e., has rank `3`.
